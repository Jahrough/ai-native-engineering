# Competency assessment framework

## Purpose

Assess whether a learner can apply a competency reliably, explain why the approach is suitable, and verify outcomes independently. Assessment is formative: it identifies next practice and does not confer a credential.

## Evidence quality

Strong evidence is:

- **Relevant:** directly maps to a stated outcome or competency.
- **Attributable:** identifies the learner's contribution and assistance used.
- **Reproducible:** includes enough safe detail to repeat or inspect the work.
- **Independently verified:** checked through tests, source inspection, review, measurement, or an external reference—not solely by the generating model.
- **Risk-aware:** documents relevant privacy, security, reliability, and failure limitations.
- **Honest:** reports uncertainty, failed attempts, scope, and what remains unimplemented.

Examples include implementation diffs and tests, specifications, controlled experiments, evaluation sets and results, threat models, runbooks, architecture decisions, and review feedback. Do not expose confidential or sensitive material in the evidence.

## Rating scale

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0 — Not demonstrated | No evidence, or evidence does not address the outcome. |
| 1 — Guided | Can perform part of the task with substantial direction; verification is incomplete. |
| 2 — Independent | Performs a bounded task independently, explains decisions, and verifies the result. |
| 3 — Adaptive | Handles ambiguity and failure, evaluates trade-offs, and adapts the method to context. |
| 4 — Systemic | Establishes repeatable practice, evaluates outcomes across a system/team, and helps others apply it safely. |

Not every competency requires rating 4. Phase guides define appropriate criteria. A rating should cite specific evidence and a date; it is not an average of tutorial completions.

## Review procedure

For each phase outcome:

1. Map one or more artifacts to the outcome.
2. Confirm the learner can explain the problem, assumptions, AI involvement, decisions, and limitations.
3. Inspect the work or reproduce the result independently; use a different verification path where practical.
4. Review relevant failure cases, security/privacy boundaries, and quality measures.
5. Assign a rating with evidence and rationale; identify a concrete next step for any gap.
6. Record reviewer and date when available, and revisit when context changes.

Self-assessment can guide study but should not be described as independent verification. Higher-risk work needs proportionate peer or domain-expert review.

## Minimum phase gate

Before marking a phase **Demonstrated**, the learner should have:

- Evidence addressing every phase outcome and required deliverable.
- At least one practical artifact or exercise, not only reading notes.
- Independent verification appropriate to the work.
- A written account of limitations, trade-offs, and remaining gaps.
- Criteria met in the phase guide, with any unresolved high-impact risk escalated rather than waived.

## Program-level portfolio review

The final review should connect baseline and reassessment, technical artifacts, evaluation results, security/operations decisions, and product architecture. It should show progression and actual outcomes without claiming more than evidence supports. Planned capstones or future integrations remain proposals until implemented and evaluated.
