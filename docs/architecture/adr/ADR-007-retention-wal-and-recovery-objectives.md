# ADR-007: Retention, WAL Capacity, and Recovery Objectives

1. **Identifier and title:** ADR-007 - Retention, WAL Capacity, and Recovery Objectives
2. **Status:** PROPOSED
3. **Date:** 2026-11-05
4. **Decision owners:** TBD - business/service owners, database owner, Kafka owner, Search owner
5. **Affected repositories:** product-catalog, product-search, platform-infrastructure, platform-gitops, platform-observability
6. **Context and problem statement:** Retention controls whether CDC can resume, events can be replayed, tombstones can fence old events, and snapshots can rebuild Search. No single retention number can be selected without workload, recovery, legal, and capacity evidence.
7. **Existing implementation and evidence:**
   - **CONFIRMED, static:** Catalog outbox includes a `processed` flag; no cleanup scheduler or retention policy was found in inspected application source. Evidence: product-catalog outbox entity/migrations and repository source.
   - **PARTIALLY CONFIRMED:** Search inbox persists event ID/version metadata; cleanup policy was not found in inspected Search source.
   - **CONFIRMED, static:** Infrastructure configures seven-day RDS backup retention. Operations warns that this is not cross-region recovery. Evidence: platform-infrastructure `terraform/modules/database/main.tf`; `OPERATIONS.md`.
   - **UNVERIFIED:** Kafka source/DLT retention, Connect internal topic retention, slot WAL growth thresholds, measured WAL generation, consumer lag, snapshot duration, and rebuild/catch-up throughput.
8. **Decision drivers:** Bound storage and recovery exposure; retain enough state for the explicitly supported replay/restore horizon; prevent cleanup from outrunning CDC; alert before a slot threatens database availability; derive RPO/RTO from business decisions and measurements.
9. **Proposed decision:** Maintain separate policies and owners for (a) business event/outbox retention, (b) Kafka source replay, (c) DLT, (d) inbox dedup/version fences, (e) tombstones, (f) PostgreSQL WAL/replication slots, and (g) backups/snapshot artifacts. Do not set numeric durations or alert thresholds in this ADR. Before selecting values, measure event rate/size, WAL generation and retained bytes, slot lag, Kafka storage/retention, outage/rebuild duration, replay horizon, maximum snapshot duration, consumer/index throughput, and restore outcomes. Cleanup must be gated by verified CDC progress and the approved recovery horizon; no slot deletion or offset reset is a routine cleanup action.
10. **Alternatives considered:** Keep all history indefinitely (simplest recovery, unbounded capacity/privacy risk); short fixed retention (bounded cost, may preclude recovery); coordinated bounded horizons plus durable tombstone/epoch fences (recommended after measurement); reconstruct all events from current Catalog snapshot only (cannot reproduce missing deletion history without tombstones).
11. **Trade-offs and consequences:** Longer retention increases storage and possibly privacy exposure; shorter retention increases recovery dependence on snapshots and makes replay unsafe. WAL retention is an availability risk, not merely an event policy. A slot can retain WAL while Kafka/Connect is unavailable; limits/monitoring and tested remediation are mandatory before production claims.
12. **Failure modes and recovery behavior:** Consumer downtime beyond Kafka retention requires a fresh snapshot/rebuild if source replay is incomplete. Slot/WAL exhaustion may block or degrade PostgreSQL and requires a tested pause/resume/re-snapshot procedure. DLT expiry loses diagnostic/replay evidence. Inbox cleanup can permit duplicate side effects unless projection version/tombstone fences remain. Tombstone purge is blocked until every supported replay/backup horizon expires or an independently durable fence exists.
13. **Security and operational considerations:** Retention must honor data minimization, legal deletion, encryption, access controls, and audit. Alert on slot retained bytes/age, WAL growth, connector progress, Kafka headroom, lag, oldest DLT age, and backup age; thresholds require baseline data and owner-defined response windows.
14. **Migration and compatibility implications:** Add measured policies, cleanup job only after safety proofs, dashboards/alarms, runbooks, and capacity tests. Keep connector offsets and replication slots outside Catalog PITR rollback authority or reconcile them under ADR-004.
15. **Dependencies on other ADRs:** ADR-002 tombstone purge; ADR-003 rebuild and snapshot duration; ADR-004 recovery epochs; ADR-006 DLT/replay; ADR-008 telemetry and operations.
16. **Acceptance criteria:** Every retained dataset has an owner, purpose, value, and deletion/recovery rule; no cleanup occurs while CDC progress is uncertain; WAL and Kafka capacity alarms are tested; a prolonged outage beyond each chosen horizon has a documented outcome; RPO/RTO values are approved and demonstrated by timed restore/rebuild exercises; values are revisited after measured workload changes.
17. **Implementation ownership:** platform-infrastructure owns DB backup/WAL/slot and Kafka retention controls; Catalog/Search own application record semantics; platform-gitops deploys workload settings; observability/operations owners report signals and act.
18. **Open questions:** What replay and backup horizons are business-required? What data deletion rules apply? What event/WAL rates and rebuild throughput are measured? Which RPO/RTO targets will owners accept? What is the remediation if WAL approaches capacity?
